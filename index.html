Raised between Finland, California and Denmark, a decade in the UK, roots now firmly back in Copenhagen. It's the reason I notice when a service assumes everyone shares one language, one address format, one way of asking for help.

Most of my work has been where getting it wrong has consequences: housing advice for people in crisis, cervical screening for the people the NHS wasn't reaching, business support for owners with no time to spare. In that world accessibility isn't a finishing step. It decides who can use the thing at all.

I start from what people do rather than what they say they'll do, a habit picked up writing a dissertation on behavioural design.

Happiest at my Finnish summerhouse, foraging for blueberries and mushrooms, and spending time with my daughter.

Flows before pixels

Requirements and journeys get settled first. Everything after that is faster, and the arguments that do happen are about the right things.

Research to focus, not to file

A ranked list of three things beats a deck of forty observations. The job isn't to find everything. It's to say what to do first.

Accessible by construction

Built into the system and the components, not audited at the end. For an audience on old devices and poor connections, it decides who gets in at all.
