Case study · Nudging

From automatic to reflective.

My MA dissertation. NHS staff know how to sort clinical waste, but at the bin, mistakes happen anyway. I wanted to find out whether nudges could close that gap.

Institution
Goldsmiths, University of London
Field site
Barts Health NHS Trust
Year
2015
Award
MA Design & Environment, Distinction

At a glance

The problem

Staff know the rules for sorting waste. Mistakes happen anyway. The gap is between knowing and doing: a two-second decision, made mid-task, by someone whose attention is on a patient.

What I did

I interviewed sustainability experts, watched what happened on the wards, surveyed staff and dug into the waste data. Then I tested it all against the EAST framework: easy, attractive, social, timely.

What changed

It changed how I work. Sorting waste happens on autopilot, but training speaks to the thinking mind. That’s why awareness campaigns don’t work.

How the work ran

  1. FrameI treated it as a behaviour problem, because staff already knew the rules.
  2. ObserveI watched what really happens at the bin, in a busy hospital.
  3. AskInterviews with sustainability experts, and surveys of the staff doing the sorting.
  4. AnalyseThe waste data and the wider context, checked against the EAST framework.

Why awareness campaigns fail

What it left me with

  • Watch what people do, as well as asking them
  • A little friction in the right place can help
  • Design for the attention people really have in the moment
  • Telling people more rarely changes what they do
