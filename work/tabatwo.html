Case study · Tabatwo

Finding focus

Tabatwo had live classes, custom sessions, instructor bookings and a social feed. The team needed to decide which of those was the product. My job was to help them choose, with evidence to back it up.

Client
Tabatwo
Role
UX Consultant
Timeline
Oct–Dec 2025
Focus
Discovery · Testing · Prioritisation

At a glance

The short version

The problem

Lots of features and no clear centre. After onboarding, people couldn't say what the app was for. Booking took too many steps, and the social features, the whole point of the brand, were tucked away.

What I did

Mapped the journeys that mattered most, ran five moderated think-aloud sessions across eight areas of the app, then ranked what we found by impact, frequency and risk to trust.

What changed

A short, ranked backlog, success criteria everyone agreed on, and a clear direction for the team at an important point in the build.

“Andrea helped us clarify fundamental product concepts that became central to our platform's direction.”

Mariano Alesandro, Co-Founder & CTO, Tabatwo

What we put in front of people

Eight areas, five sessions

How the work ran

Five steps over three months

  1. FrameThe brief was “improve the app”. First I sat down with the founders and wrote one sentence about what Tabatwo is for: training with people you know, easily. That sentence settled every later argument about priorities.
  2. MapI mapped the journeys that mattered most: onboarding, booking an instructor and starting a workout. It showed the mess early: three overlapping ways to book and two ways into search.
  3. TestFive moderated think-aloud sessions of 60 to 90 minutes, with people aged 21 to 68, across eight areas of the app.
  4. RankI grouped the findings into themes and ranked them by impact, how often they came up, and risk to trust. The team got a short list of what to fix first.
  5. Hand overWe agreed what “fixed” would look like: understand the app in 30 seconds, book in under three taps. The handover was a working session with product and engineering.

What testing found

High impact first

Onboarding

High impact

What we saw

  • Payment details were requested before users understood what the app offers
  • Confusion between “fitness level” and “activity level”
  • Concerns about being asked age, weight and gender
  • OTP took longer than ten seconds

Recommended

  • Collect only what you will use, and later
  • Reorder the goals; move “Skip” to the top right
  • Explain why each question is asked

Home screen

High impact

What we saw

  • No clear call to action, and no personalised content
  • Users wanted a guiding hand on first open

Recommended

  • Decide the single main CTA
  • Add a “Let’s get started” prompt for first-time users
  • Rename “Bookmarks” to “Saved”

Custom session

High impact

What we saw

  • Only one of five users managed to play a saved session
  • Users had to calculate session length themselves
  • Too little distinction between work and rest screens
  • Playback issues caused real demotivation

Recommended

  • Add a play button for saved sessions
  • Audio guidance and a beginner overview up front
  • Clear visual difference between active and rest

Payment

High impact

What we saw

  • Unclear what was free and what was paid
  • Uncertainty over whether invited friends pay too
  • The Apple payment disclaimer read as untrustworthy

Recommended

  • State up front that the app is free unless booking an instructor
  • Move payment details to profile settings

Friends & community

High impact

What we saw

  • “My friends” and “Community” overlapped
  • No way to preview a profile before adding
  • Posting felt like a less intuitive Instagram; messaging was preferred

Recommended

  • Invite a friend during booking, not after
  • Reconsider feed-style sharing versus group chats
  • Let instructors be followed

Navigation

Medium

What we saw

  • Back button sometimes returned home, risking lost progress
  • Search was hard to find
  • “+” sat where the profile icon was expected

Recommended

  • One consistent position for search and profile
  • Unified search defaulting to “All”
  • Review the main menu

Booking an instructor

Medium

What we saw

  • All five booked successfully, but “Book this instructor” sat at the very bottom
  • “Book slot”, “request slot” and “custom request” were confused
  • Friends could only be invited after booking

Recommended

  • Move “Book now” to the top
  • Three steps or fewer
  • Invite friends earlier in the flow

Calendar

Medium

What we saw

  • Time scrolling moved minute by minute
  • No week view
  • Weeks starting on Sunday caused friction

Recommended

  • Scroll in fifteen-minute intervals
  • Add a month/week toggle

How it was ranked

Impact against frequency

What to fix first

In this order

  1. 01

    Decide what the app is for

    Instructors, or friends and online workouts? Pick one and make that flow great. Until then, every other decision was guesswork.

  2. 02

    Guide the first two minutes

    Confusion in onboarding cost trust and motivation before the app had a chance to show what it does.

  3. 03

    Book in three taps

    Book, pick a time, confirm. Put “Book now” at the top, and let people invite friends earlier.

  4. 04

    Put friends at the centre

    Training with friends is the heart of the brand, and it was the most hidden part of the app.

What success looks like

Agreed with product and engineering

  • Users understand the app within 30 seconds
  • The home screen guides you to a first action
  • Booking takes fewer than three taps
  • Workouts run reliably, without friction
  • Social features feel integrated, not hidden
  • Beginners feel supported and included
