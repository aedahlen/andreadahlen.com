Tabatwo

Defining the core UX of a social fitness platform — helping the team clarify what the product was really about, and designing around that focus.

Client
Tabatwo
Role
UX Consultant
Timeline
Oct — Dec 2025
Focus
Discovery, testing, prioritisation

Overview

Tabatwo is a social fitness platform designed to help people train together, either through live instructor-led sessions or shared workouts with friends. I joined during a critical phase of product development, where core features were being defined and validated ahead of wider rollout.

My role was to lead user-centred design across key product areas, making sure foundational concepts were clear, usable and aligned with both user needs and technical constraints — driving UX from discovery through validation, working closely with product and engineering to support fast, informed decisions.

The challenge

Tabatwo offered a broad range of functionality — onboarding, workouts, instructors, scheduling and social features — but the experience lacked a clear centre of gravity.

  • Users struggled to understand the core value proposition early on
  • High-friction flows around booking sessions and starting workouts
  • Social features felt disconnected from the main experience
  • Complexity and inconsistency across navigation and language
  • Early technical issues affecting trust and motivation

The challenge wasn't to improve individual screens. It was to help the team clarify what the product was really about, and design around that focus.

My role & responsibilities

  • Leading design of core product features: social workouts, custom sessions and playback, instructor booking and scheduling
  • Defining user flows and functional requirements before visual design
  • Planning and moderating usability testing with end users
  • Synthesising insights into clear, prioritised recommendations
  • Collaborating closely with product and engineering on feasibility and alignment
  • Supporting faster, more confident delivery in a fast-moving environment

Approach

A structured, end-to-end process balancing discovery, validation and delivery.

  1. 01

    Discovery & flow definition

    Before any visual execution, I mapped key user journeys and identified high-value actions — particularly onboarding, booking, and starting a workout. This surfaced complexity early and aligned the team on what needed testing.

  2. 02

    Usability testing

    I planned and moderated task-based, think-aloud testing with five users aged 21–68, covering onboarding, the home screen, custom sessions, instructor booking, calendar use and social features. Sessions ran 60–90 minutes.

  3. 03

    Synthesis & prioritisation

    Findings were synthesised into themes and prioritised by impact, frequency and risk. Rather than hand over a long list of issues, I focused on helping the team understand what to fix first — especially where friction affected trust, motivation, or revenue.

First impressions

What landed well

  • Comfortable, neutral colour palette
  • Calm, simple design
  • Appealing overall look
  • Genuine excitement about training with friends conveniently

What didn't

  • Alignment issues
  • Excessive white space
  • Small typos and spacing errors made the app feel less polished
  • The concept needed more clarity in execution

Findings by area

The full breakdown from the usability sessions, area by area.

Onboarding

Findings

  • Generally intuitive, and not overly long or boring
  • Concerns around age, weight and gender — careful not to discriminate or put users off
  • Confusion between “fitness level” and “activity level”
  • Payment information was requested before users understood what the app offers
  • OTP took longer than 10 seconds to generate

Recommendations

  • Reduce steps and only collect information you plan to use
  • Consider requesting a profile image
  • Review the order of goals — “Gain weight” first?
  • Move “Skip” to the top right

Homescreen

Findings

  • Users expected personalised content based on their preferences
  • Curiosity about the app's offering, but no clear call to action
  • Users wanted an introduction or a guiding hand

Recommendations

  • Decide on the main CTA and add a “Let's get started” prompt for first-time users
  • Improve the structure of the screen
  • Change “Bookmarks” to “Saved” — more modern terminology

Navigation

Findings

  • Inconsistent back button — sometimes returns home rather than the previous screen, risking lost progress
  • Excessive white space, prompting unnecessary scrolling
  • Search was difficult to find
  • “+” for adding friends sat where profile settings were expected

Recommendations

  • Consistent UI for search and profile icons
  • Unified search, defaulting to “All”
  • Review the main menu

Custom session

Findings

  • Users had to calculate session length themselves
  • Preselected workouts were inconsistent — sometimes three, sometimes none
  • Only one user managed to play a saved session
  • Exercise videos lacked explanations for beginners, and not all users knew the Tabata concept
  • On-screen text too small; too little distinction between workout and rest screens
  • Play and pause controls shifted position; stop, end and skip were unclear
  • Video playback issues caused real demotivation

Recommendations

  • Add audio guidance
  • Make custom workouts beginner-friendly with an overview up front
  • Clearer indicators between rest and active periods
  • Quick win: add a play button for saved sessions and increase font size

Booking an instructor

Findings

  • All five users successfully booked a session
  • “Book this instructor” was hard to find, sitting at the very bottom
  • Confusion between “book slot”, “request slot” and “custom request”
  • Booking required too many steps
  • Time and price were the information users cared about most
  • Unclear when an instructor was unavailable
  • Inviting friends came too late in the process

Recommendations

  • Filter or organise instructors by specialty, or alphabetically
  • Move “Book now” to the top
  • Keep booking to three steps or fewer
  • Let friends be invited earlier in the flow
  • Use consistent language throughout

Payment

Findings

  • Users expected pricing information earlier in the experience
  • Lack of clarity over free versus paid features
  • Unclear whether invited friends would also need to pay
  • Mistrust in the payment process — the Apple payment disclaimer was confusing and off-putting

Recommendations

  • Advertise that the app is free unless booking instructors; move payment info to profile settings
  • Consider not asking for payment details during profile setup

Calendar

Findings

  • Month view worked, but users expected to toggle to a week view
  • Time scrolling was too sensitive, moving minute by minute
  • Users expected to see instructor availability for a specific day
  • Friction with weeks starting on a Sunday

Recommendations

  • Scroll time slots in 15-minute intervals
  • Add a month and week view toggle

Friends & community

Findings

  • Difficulty finding where to add a friend — placed where a profile icon is expected
  • Users wanted to preview a friend's profile before adding them
  • One user expected to be paired with training partners rather than inviting existing friends
  • “My friends” and “Community” overlapped in function
  • Posting resembled Instagram but felt less intuitive; private messaging was strongly preferred

Recommendations

  • Integrate friends into the core experience — invite a friend during booking, not after
  • Reconsider whether Instagram-like sharing is needed, versus group chats or stories
  • Let instructors promote workouts, or be followed

Priorities

  • Decide on the main offering — instructors, or friends and online workouts — and perfect that flow first
  • Simplify onboarding so first-time users are guided; confusion in the first two minutes costs trust and motivation
  • Simplify booking to book → select time → confirm
  • Clarify pricing earlier in the journey
  • Integrate community and social features into the core experience — the social proposition is central to the brand but under-leveraged
  • Improve the workout session itself: video reliability, audio, playback controls and rest screens

Outcomes

  • Clearer product direction during a pivotal development phase
  • Better alignment between design, product and engineering
  • Reduced ambiguity around core user journeys
  • A shared understanding of what success should look like from a user's perspective

What success looks like

  • Users understand the app within 30 seconds
  • The home screen guides users to their first action
  • Booking takes fewer than three taps
  • Workouts run reliably, without friction
  • Social features feel integrated, not hidden
  • Beginners feel safe, supported and included

Reflections

  • Using research to create focus, not just to find problems
  • Prioritising clarity and momentum over feature breadth
  • Embedding a product's value proposition directly into the UX
  • Acting as a facilitator and strategic partner, not only a designer
