Case study · Shelter

Housing advice when the rules changed every week.

In March 2020 Shelter's face-to-face hubs closed just as demand for housing advice rose. People needed answers on their phones, and the law behind those answers changed almost daily. I was Senior UX Designer in the cross-functional team that designed Shelter's digital response.

At a glance

The short version

The problem

Situations nobody had advised on before: two rents because of a travel ban, repairs you couldn't let into the house, housemates who wanted you out. Face-to-face help had closed, and 75% of visits were on mobile.

What I did

Designed how people reached one emergency advice page, kept a fast-growing page usable on a phone, and used search, analytics and feedback to decide what to change next.

What changed

1.5 million pageviews, a quarter of all traffic to the site. A rent negotiation page built from user feedback became the most clicked next step.

My part

One discipline in a team of many

I owned the user experience of the advice journey: how people found the page, how they moved through it on a phone, and what they did next. I worked from site search data, the analytics dashboard and Hotjar feedback, and brought what I found to our daily stand-up.

I worked alongside Marida Maiorino, our Senior Service Designer, who led the research synthesis, and Catherine Dickinson and the content team, who wrote and kept the advice up to date. The decisions below were made together.

How the work ran

Five steps, repeated for months

  1. ListenDaily analysis of helpline and webchat conversations.
  2. StructureOne page for many audiences, built to be scanned. Live 19 March.
  3. RouteA global banner, so nobody had to search for the advice.
  4. MeasureSmall, evidence-backed changes over untested new patterns.
  5. ExtendA rent negotiation letter, built from what people asked for next.

Getting people to the page

Search told us where to look

Choosing the safe change

Long page, small screens

Considered: an accordion

Every section visible at a glance, opened one at a time. A shorter page on a phone, but a new pattern for our advice and untested with people under stress.

Shipped: small changes, backed by data

Sections reordered by demand, urgency and risk. Changes checked against the analytics dashboard, and weekly catch-ups with housing advisers on what their clients faced.

What I took from it

  1. Mobile first, for realThree in four people read the advice on a phone, often in a hurry and under stress. Every decision about length, order and navigation started there.
  2. Numbers and stories need each otherSearch data told us what was common. The conversations told us what was urgent. We needed both to set priorities.
  3. The best next step is something to doThe most clicked link from the advice page was a letter people could send. Advice became useful once it turned into an action.
