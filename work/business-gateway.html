Redesigning Business Gateway

Over four years, features had been added to the site one section at a time. The new site separated its two main audiences, start-ups and growing businesses, and gave each of them their own route.

At a glance

The problem

Functionality had accumulated piecemeal, section by section, for four years. Start-ups and growing businesses were served the same undifferentiated site, and neither found what they came for.

What I did

Lean and agile: research into a structural argument, co-creation workshops, wireframes before visual design, then two UX, two design and four development sprints.

What changed

An architecture that separates start-up from growth, a tool that tailors content to your stage, sector and place, and a WAI ‘A’ rating.

“Andrea is a smart, strategic thinker who I've had the pleasure working with on several complex web build projects at Whitespace.”

Sarah Ormerod, Account Director, Whitespace

Why it needed rebuilding

The site had performed well for four years. New features and content had been added successfully, but always to individual sections, never across the whole site. Each addition made sense on its own, but together the site became harder to use.

What was left was a structure that had grown rather than been designed: two very different audiences moving through one undifferentiated site, help sitting behind gates, and local offices running as separate micro-sites outside the thing they belonged to. Adding one more section would not have fixed it.

What the research found

Growth businessesHigh impact+

What the research showed

  • Perceived Business Gateway as a business generalist, not a specialist
  • Wanted a tailored service from people who understood their sector
  • Highly self-reliant and unused to asking outside services for help
  • Sought directional information: market reports, research, sector data

What we changed

  • A dedicated Your Sector area serving industry-specific content
  • Market reports and research services surfaced, not buried under Services
  • Specialist framing rather than general business advice
Start-upsMedium+

What the research showed

  • Looking for help, advice, networking and best practice
  • Actionable 'how to' content had performed well on the old site
  • Time-poor, and wanting content brought to them rather than searched for

What we changed

  • More actionable how-to guides
  • Popular guides served straight from the gateway
  • A personalisation tool that returns what applies to you
Content and gatingHigh impact+

What the research showed

  • Content sat behind gates that blocked the people it was written for
  • Guides were buried in a rigid hierarchy
  • Tone did not match an audience that needed motivating

What we changed

  • Gated content removed
  • Filters in place of hierarchical navigation
  • Industry experts brought in for real case studies and advice
  • An inspiring, motivating tone of voice
Local officesHigh impact+

What the research showed

  • Local offices ran as exterior micro-sites, outside the main experience
  • Users responding to an advert landed somewhere disconnected from the offering

What we changed

  • Local offices fully integrated into the main site
  • Case studies and inspiration grouped under a new Success Stories gateway

A new information architecture

  1. Organise by what people are trying to do.Starting up and running a business became the top-level division inside Online Resources.
  2. Put case studies in one place.Case studies and inspiration stories were scattered. Success Stories gave them one home and made them findable.
  3. Add Your Sector.The answer to growth businesses seeing Business Gateway as a generalist: content specific to the industry you actually operate in.
  4. Move the local offices into the main site.They had been exterior micro-sites. Integrating them meant an advert could land someone somewhere connected to the rest of the offering.

From wireframe to build

The tailored tool

The audience research kept returning the same two things: people were time-poor, and they wanted content that already knew something about them. So we made a tool that asks three optional questions: what stage your business is at, what sector it is in and where you are based. It then shows only the resources, events and reports that match.

I kept it short, optional and in plain language. It is the part of the project I am most proud of.

How the work ran

  1. ReviewStarted from an existing UX review and audience research, and used it to plan the new structure.
  2. SeparateEstablished that start-up and growth are different audiences with different jobs.
  3. Co-createWorkshops with the client while the architecture was still movable.
  4. WireframeLots of wireframes, before anyone opened a design tool.
  5. ShipTwo UX sprints, two design sprints, four development sprints.

What it did

392%Rise in market report downloads after the path was redesigned
85%Completed the tailored tool and clicked a resource it returned
20.5%More goals completed on site than before
23%Rise in pages per session on mobile, with bounce rate down

Across all devices, pages per session rose from 3.81 to 4.05. The site was delivered to a WAI ‘A’ rating, with passes against many AA and AAA criteria.

What I would do differently

We worked at pace and co-created with the client, and the budget left very little room for formal user testing. Without testing with real users, it was hard to know if we were right. I would rather make changes based on evidence than on agreement in the room.

It still worked because we weren't designing alone. The client had a lot of user insight, and we also had the UX review and the audience research. The results show that. But working with the client doesn't replace testing, and I would push harder for a testing budget now.

The other unfinished piece is accessibility. WAI ‘A’ with many AA and AAA passes was a good start, but not the finish. Reducing the number of PDFs and closing the gap to full AA compliance was the work I wanted to carry into the next phase.
