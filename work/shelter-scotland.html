Leading Shelter Scotland's digital team

As Product Lead, I managed a team of eight and was responsible for Shelter Scotland's website. I planned the team's work across advice, campaigns and fundraising, and made accessibility part of how we worked.

Deciding what to take on

The digital team worked for the whole of Shelter Scotland. Each area had its own deadlines and priorities, and they often needed us at the same time.

EventsFundraisingCampaignsHousing adviceHelpline

I set up a prioritisation framework, so we decided what to take on in the same way each time. It also helped keep the other teams satisfied, because they could see why something was or wasn't on the roadmap. These were the questions we asked:

  1. What user need does it meet?And what evidence, from data or research, shows the need is real?
  2. Which of Shelter Scotland's goals does it help?And how much does it matter to the people asking for it?
  3. Does something like it already exist?Sometimes the answer was to improve what we had, not build something new.
  4. Do we have the time and skills?If it clashed with other planned work, we said no or not yet. Knowing how to say no was part of the job.

The digital roadmap

Before, the team didn't have a roadmap. I created one, so it was clear what we were working on, when, and what our goals were. I planned the work quarter by quarter, managed the budget, and looked after several product streams at the same time.

I shared progress through quarterly reports and show and tells, and the team planned its work in sprints, with stand-ups, retros and a Kanban board.

I kept the Head of Communications and Engagement involved as the roadmap took shape, so it matched Shelter Scotland's goals, and kept in touch with the wider Shelter digital team to avoid doing the same work twice.

Accessibility

Many people come to Shelter Scotland at a stressful time, and need to understand their housing rights quickly. I championed accessibility across the team, and the Scottish website met WCAG 2.1 AA.

That included the way the housing advice is written. We worked on guidance for writing accessible content, so that the advice can be understood by as many people as possible. We tested the advice with users, and rewrote guides as steps, with clear actions and next steps. This reduced calls to the helpline.

A repairs guide on the Scottish website, on desktop and on a phone. The guide is split into numbered steps, with a short summary and a clear next step.
