Case study · Shelter & Shelter Scotland

A rebrand in rough red brushstroke, made repeatable.

Shelter's rebrand was meant to look urgent and unpolished. My job was to turn it into components a team could use every week, and make sure people in crisis could still read the advice.

Shelter & Shelter Scotland

At a glance

The short version

The problem

The new brand was a rough red brushstroke, raw on purpose. It had to become interface components people could read and teams could reuse. And the brand red failed contrast for links.

What I did

I built the pattern library: every colour named with a job, specs for three screen sizes, and a Storybook library developers could build from. Then I led Shelter Scotland's digital team.

What changed

WCAG 2.1 AA across the Scottish site, more people able to read the housing advice, and a team of eight working from one shared system.

The brand, as it arrived

Loud on purpose

How the work ran

Five steps over five years

  1. AuditThe rebrand was made for posters, not for a form field on a small phone. I went through the site screen by screen and listed where the brushstroke, the type and the red stopped working. That list became the brief.
  2. EncodeEvery colour got a name and a job. The brand red couldn't carry links at accessible contrast, so links got their own blue, with set colours for error, success, alert and disabled.
  3. SpecifyComponent specs for desktop, tablet and mobile, including when not to use each one. Written so content designers and developers could follow them too.
  4. BuildA Storybook library, so developers built from the live system instead of a screenshot. Accessibility lived inside the components, so it shipped by default.
  5. LeadAs Product Lead I ran a team of eight: a UX designer, a senior developer, a user researcher and five content designers. I also owned the roadmap, budget, training and upkeep of the system.
