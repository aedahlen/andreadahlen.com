Case study · Shelter & Shelter Scotland

A rebrand in rough red brushstroke, made repeatable.

Shelter's rebrand was deliberately urgent and unpolished. My job was to turn that energy into components a team could ship every week, at a standard that let people in crisis actually read the advice.

Client
Shelter & Shelter Scotland
Role

At a glance

If you read nothing else · 40 seconds

The problem

A brand built on a rough red brushstroke, deliberately raw, had to become legible, accessible, repeatable interface components. The brand red could not carry link states at accessible contrast.

What I did

Built the pattern library and design system: named colour variables with defined roles, component specs across three breakpoints, and a Storybook library developers could theme against. Then led the Scottish digital estate.

What changed

WCAG 2.1 AA across the estate, a wider audience reaching housing advice, and a team of eight shipping against one system instead of individual judgement.

01 · Audit

The rebrand was designed for posters and protest, not for a form field at 320px. I went through the estate screen by screen and catalogued where the brushstroke, the type and the red stopped working — long advice pages, error states, anything a person reads under stress. That catalogue was the brief for the system.

Colour with a job

Encoded once, not argued per screen

Brand red

Campaigns and emphasis. Deliberately loud, and kept away from anything a reader has to parse under stress.

Link blue

Text links and their states. Introduced because the brand red could not carry them at accessible contrast.

Error / success / alert

Defined state colours, each with a role rather than a preference.

Disabled

One value, one meaning, so the same control reads the same everywhere.
